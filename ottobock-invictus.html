Making an essential support role visible.

Ottobock's technicians were the pit crew behind every athlete at the 2017 Invictus Games. Almost nobody outside the venue knew they existed.

01 The problem

An indispensable role, structurally invisible.

Ottobock ran the on-site repair and fitting workshop at the Games. When a prosthetic socket cracked between heats or a racing chair lost alignment, a technician fixed it in minutes. Without them, athletes would not have competed. And yet the role sat entirely outside the story the Games were telling - which was, correctly, about the athletes.

The constraint

A components manufacturer cannot make itself the protagonist of an adaptive-sport event without looking opportunistic. The audience did not want a technology story and the media were not going to run one.

Nor could the brand claim credit for athlete performance. The performance belonged to the athletes.

The strategic move

Stop pitching the technology. Pitch the relationship - the technician and the athlete, and what the technology makes possible in a life rather than in a race.

That reframe gave reporters two things they actually wanted: a human character nobody had written about, and a credible expert to explain the technology inside an athlete's story.

The story platform

Technology matters because it supports real people, real performance, and real independence.

One sentence that resolved the whole problem. It gave every pitch a subject the brand could legitimately own, put the athlete first, and made the technician - not the product - the way in.

02 Objectives

Three things the program had to accomplish.

  1. 01

    Show the service role

    Make on-site repair and fitting legible as a story, not a logistics footnote.

  2. 02

    Elevate athlete stories

    Lead with the competitor. The brand earns relevance by being useful to their narrative, not by interrupting it.

  3. 03

    Equip spokespeople

    Prepare technicians, executives, and athletes to handle on-site media conversations confidently and consistently.

What the numbers show

An 8:1 ratio between original stories and total pickup. That multiplier is the signature of stories built to be syndicated - wire-friendly human narratives with a technical expert attached - rather than announcements pushed to a list.

Coverage landed in People, ESPN, CBC News, CTV News, the Toronto Star, the National Post, and the Discovery Channel.

What I would do with it now

Reach and shares were the 2017 currency and they are no longer sufficient. If I ran this today I would define success as share of adaptive-technology conversation and message penetration - what proportion of coverage actually carried the service-role narrative rather than simply naming the brand.

That is a measurable question, and it is the one a client should have been asking.
